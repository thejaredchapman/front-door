Research date: **2026-11-07**. Benchmark: **Freddie Mac PMMS 30-yr fixed = 7.18%, 15-yr = 6.57% (week of 2026-11-05)**, confirmed from `https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms/docs/PMMS_history.csv` (last row `11/5/2026,7.18,,6.57`). Machine-readable companion: `docs/research/cheapest-lenders.json`.

**Ground rules used here**
- A rate or fee is listed only if a page showed it, and it comes with that page's date and URL. "No date on page" means the page gave no effective date.
- **UNVERIFIED** means the claim came from a search snippet or a third party, or the page was stale or wouldn't load.
- Published "as low as" rates assume strong borrowers, often a 740–780 FICO and 60–75% LTV. A low-income first-time buyer with 3–3.5% down and a 620–700 score will be quoted higher, plus mortgage insurance. Use these numbers to rank lenders, not to predict anyone's actual quote.

### What this means

1. **For a member with 3% down, the cheapest fixed-rate option found is a portfolio "no-PMI" credit-union loan.** SchoolsFirst's School Employee Special has a 7.19% APR with no mortgage insurance. Compare a standard 3%-down conventional loan: PMI on a 97% LTV loan at a ~700 FICO often adds about 0.5–1.0%/yr of the loan amount. That PMI range is a general market figure, **UNVERIFIED** for any specific lender. On a $600k loan, it's roughly $250–$500/month that the no-PMI product avoids.
2. **FHA is the low-credit-score path, and it costs more in total than the rate suggests.** SchoolsFirst's FHA 30-yr is 7.000% at zero points, but its APR is 7.78% once MI is included. HMDA confirms this cost gap (section 3): OC FHA purchase loans in 2024 had median total loan costs of about **$22k–$25k** at the largest FHA lenders, because the 1.75% upfront MIP counts as a loan cost. Conventional loans at the same lenders ran about **$8k–$11k**.
3. **By HMDA rate spread in OC, credit unions and some banks price below the big nonbank lenders.** In 2024, SchoolsFirst (354 loans) had a median spread of -0.03 and median total loan costs of about $5.7k. Rocket and UWM ran +0.13 to +0.19 spread with costs of $8.2k–$11k. Several brokers and non-QM lenders ran +0.5 to +1.4.
4. **The lowest-spread names in HMDA mostly serve high-income clients**, e.g. Morgan Stanley Private Bank, Schwab, City National. Builder lenders (Lennar, Pulte) also look cheap because builders buy down the rate. None of these is a realistic, repeatable option for a low-income first-time buyer shopping resale homes.

### Lender types, ranked by typical total cost for an LMI first-time buyer (judgment, based on the evidence above)

1. **Credit unions with portfolio no-PMI or first-time-buyer products** you are eligible to join: SchoolsFirst, Firefighters First, Navy Federal, Orange County's CU, Golden 1, and others.
2. **Down-payment-assistance-paired loans through nonprofit or CDFI lenders and CalHFA lenders** (NeighborWorks OC, city programs). These are cheapest in cash-to-close, often not in rate. See section 5.
3. **Mortgage brokers (wholesale)**. They have competitive par rates on agency/FHA loans, but HMDA shows wide variance between brokers. Compare the Loan Estimate, not the pitch.
4. **Large retail banks**. They are competitive for strong-credit borrowers. Look for their LMI-specific grants, which are not documented here and are **UNVERIFIED**.
5. **Big online nonbanks** (Rocket, UWM channel, loanDepot). They sit mid-pack in OC HMDA spreads and are convenient. Shop them as one of the quotes.
6. **Non-QM, hard-money, and seller financing**. Most expensive. Use only when nothing else works.

## 5. Practical shopping guidance for a low-income OC buyer

1. **Get 3–5 Loan Estimates on the same day**, with the same loan type, down payment, and lock period. The app already says this.
   - CFPB: "Within a 45-day window, multiple credit checks from mortgage lenders are recorded on your credit report as a single inquiry." Source: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-exactly-happens-when-a-mortgage-lender-checks-my-credit-en-2005/
   - The app's "14–45 days" phrasing is accurate across scoring models. CFPB uses 45.
2. **Include at least one each of these lender types:**
   - A credit union you can join. OC residents can generally join Orange County's CU, Wescom, Golden 1, and California CU by geography; SchoolsFirst, Navy Federal, and Firefighters First by affiliation.
   - A CalHFA or NeighborWorks lender, if you need DPA.
   - A broker.
   - One big online lender.
3. **Compare all of these, not just the rate:**
   - Loan Estimate page 2, Section A (origination charges + points, or a negative lender credit).
   - Mortgage insurance per month.
   - Upfront MIP or funding fee.
   - Section J lender credits.
   - Cash to close.
   - The APR (APR includes MI on FHA and on PMI loans in SchoolsFirst's sheet).
4. **Points vs lender credits.** SchoolsFirst's sheet shows the trade directly:
   - 1 point buys about 0.25% off the rate.
   - -1 point (a credit) costs about +0.25%.
   - On a $500k loan, 1 point = $5,000 to save about $80/month. Breakeven is about 5 years.
   - Low-cash buyers who may refinance or move usually do better taking zero points or a lender credit. The app's `QuoteComparer` already models this.
5. **No-PMI products beat low rates.** A 3%-down conventional loan with PMI can cost more per month than a slightly higher-rate no-PMI credit-union loan. Compare monthly payment including MI.
6. **Broker vs direct.**
   - Brokers can reach wholesale pricing, and UWM's channel is mid-pack on spread in OC HMDA. Broker compensation is built into price or fees.
   - Direct credit unions can't shop other lenders but may offer portfolio products (no-PMI, 0% down ARM) that brokers can't.
7. **DPA and CalHFA costs.**
   - CalPLUS (with ZIP) carries a "slightly higher" first-mortgage rate than standard CalHFA.
   - MyHome/MyAccess are 1% deferred junior loans, repaid on sale or refinance.
   - Dream For All is shared appreciation: you repay principal plus a share of the home's gain.
   - City programs (Garden Grove, Santa Ana "My First Home") and the County MAP are silent seconds with resale/occupancy restrictions. Amounts are **UNVERIFIED** here.
   - Ask every lender to quote both with and without DPA.
8. **ARM caution.** Several of the cheapest low-down options are ARMs:
   - Firefighters First 7/1 FTHB.
   - OC CU 0%-down ARM.
   - California CU's $10k no-closing-cost ARM.
   They are cheaper now, but the payment can rise after 5–7 years (e.g. Firefighters First: 2%/5% caps). Qualify at the fully-indexed rate.
